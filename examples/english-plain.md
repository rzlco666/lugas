# English Plain — Examples

Full before/after rewrites. Each example shows a longer passage, not a one-liner, with a short note on what changed. Use these as a style reference when writing or reviewing.

---

## 1. Error page — payment failed

**Before:**

> We regret to inform you that your payment could not be processed at this time due to insufficient funds in the account associated with your payment method. Please ensure that your payment method is valid and that sufficient funds are available, and try again at a later date. If the problem persists, please do not hesitate to contact our customer support team, who will be happy to assist you with this matter.

**After:**

> Your payment did not go through. There was not enough money in the account.
>
> Try a different card. Or add money to the account and try again.
>
> If it still does not work, contact support. Quote code P-201 so we can help faster.

**What changed:** The cause is named in plain words (*not enough money* instead of *insufficient funds*). The reader gets two concrete next steps instead of a vague *try again later*. The support line gives a code to quote, so the reader is not left guessing what to say.

## 2. Welcome email — new account

**Before:**

> Welcome to our cutting-edge platform! We are delighted to have you on board and we look forward to supporting you on your journey. Please do not hesitate to explore our world-class features at your leisure. In order to get started, kindly navigate to your dashboard and familiarise yourself with the various functionalities available to you. Our team is here to assist you every step of the way.

**After:**

> Welcome to Northwind.
>
> Here is how to start:
>
> 1. Add your first project.
> 2. Invite your team.
> 3. Create your first report.
>
> Each step takes about 2 minutes. If you get stuck, read the 5-minute guide: [Get started with Northwind](https://example.com/start).

**What changed:** Clichés (*cutting-edge, world-class, delighted to have you on board*) are gone. The email gives three numbered steps instead of praise. The link text describes its destination, and the time cost is honest.

## 3. Public-service notice — road closure

**Before:**

> Please be advised that, due to unforeseen circumstances, the aforementioned road will be temporarily closed to all vehicular traffic until further notice. Motorists are advised to utilise alternative routes. We apologise for any inconvenience this may cause and thank you for your understanding.

**After:**

> Market Street will be closed to cars from Monday 8 June to Friday 12 June, 7 a.m. to 6 p.m. Jakarta time (WIB).
>
> Use River Road instead. Buses will still run on Market Street.
>
> We are sorry for the disruption. For updates, call 021 555 0142.

**What changed:** Exact dates, times, and time zone replace *until further notice*. The alternative route is named. The apology is real and specific, and help is a phone number, not a vague *thank you for your understanding*.

## 4. Step-by-step guide — resetting a password

**Before:**

> In the event that you have forgotten your password, you may utilise the password reset functionality by clicking on the link provided below, subsequent to which you will receive an email containing instructions on how to proceed. Please ensure that you follow the instructions carefully in order to successfully complete the process.

**After:**

> **How to reset your password**
>
> Before you start, you need access to the email address on your account.
>
> 1. Select **Forgot password?** on the sign-in page.
> 2. Enter your email address.
> 3. Open the email from us and select the reset link.
> 4. Choose a new password with at least 8 characters.
>
> **Done?** You can sign in with your new password. The link expires after 1 hour.

**What changed:** The guide starts with a clear heading and a prerequisite. Each step starts with a verb and does one thing. *In the event that*, *utilise*, *subsequent to*, and *in order to* are all gone. The reader can check whether it worked.

## 5. Report findings — user research

**Before:**

> It is important to note that a considerable number of users experienced difficulties with regard to the utilisation of the new functionality, which resulted in a significant decrease in overall satisfaction levels. Furthermore, a large number of respondents indicated that they were unable to locate the relevant feature, notwithstanding the fact that it was prominently displayed on the main dashboard.

**After:**

> Many users found the new search feature hard to use. Satisfaction fell from 80% to 62%.
>
> Of the 40 people we interviewed, 28 could not find the feature. It is on the main dashboard, but the label says "Explore". People expected it to say "Search".
>
> We recommend renaming the button to "Search" and testing it with 10 more users.

**What changed:** Vague words (*considerable, significant, a large number*) became numbers (80% to 62%, 28 of 40). One idea per sentence. The finding ends with a concrete recommendation, not just a complaint.

## 6. Terms and conditions — cancellation

**Before:**

> The aforementioned party shall be liable for any and all charges incurred pursuant to the utilisation of the service, including but not limited to any fees, taxes, or surcharges as may be applicable. Cancellation of the service must be effected by the subscriber no less than 30 days prior to the renewal date, failing which the subscription shall be deemed to have been renewed for a further period of 12 months.

**After:**

> You pay for what you use. That includes fees and taxes.
>
> To cancel, contact us at least 30 days before your renewal date. Your renewal date is 4 June 2026.
>
> If you cancel after that date, your plan runs for another 12 months.

**What changed:** *The aforementioned party* became *you*. The renewal date is stated, so the reader does not have to look it up. The consequence of late cancellation is one short sentence, not a nested clause.

## 7. Health — appointment reminder

**Before:**

> This is a reminder that your appointment is scheduled for the aforementioned date and time. Please ensure you arrive 15 minutes prior to your scheduled appointment time and bring your identification documents with you. Failure to attend may result in the cancellation of your appointment and the imposition of a fee.

**After:**

> Your appointment is on Tuesday 9 June at 10 a.m. Jakarta time (WIB).
>
> Please arrive 15 minutes early. Bring your ID card.
>
> If you cannot come, call 021 555 0142 at least 1 day before. If you do not call, we may charge a fee of Rp 100,000.

**What changed:** The exact date and time are in the first sentence. *Prior to* became *early*. The consequence is specific (the fee amount) and the reader is told how to avoid it.

## 8. Refusal — feature request turned down

**Before:**

> Thank you for your valuable feedback. After careful consideration, we regret to inform you that we are unable to accommodate your request at this juncture, due to competing priorities and resource constraints. We hope you understand that this decision was not taken lightly, and we would like to assure you that your feedback has been noted for future reference.

**After:**

> Thank you for asking for a dark mode.
>
> We cannot add it before July. Our team is finishing the new payment flow first.
>
> As an alternative, you can lower the screen brightness in Settings. Would that help for now?

**What changed:** The refusal is in the second paragraph, plainly stated, with a real reason and a date. The stock phrases (*we regret to inform you, not taken lightly, noted for future reference*) are gone. The reader gets an alternative and a question, not a closed door.

## 9. Onboarding — first-run screen

**Before:**

> Congratulations on joining our community! You are now part of a growing network of users who are leveraging our platform to achieve their goals. To commence your journey, please take a moment to complete your profile, explore the dashboard, and familiarise yourself with the various tools at your disposal.

**After:**

> Welcome. You are in.
>
> First, add your name and photo. This helps your team recognise you.
>
> Then, create your first project. It takes about 2 minutes.
>
> You can skip any step and come back later.

**What changed:** The greeting is two words, not a paragraph of praise. Two steps, each with a reason and a time estimate. The reader is told they can skip — user control, stated plainly.

## 10. Notification — report ready

**After:**

> Your monthly report is ready.
>

## 11. Help article — changing an email address

**Before:**

> In order to change the email address associated with your account, the user should navigate to the settings page and subsequently click on the button labelled "Account". Thereafter, the user should input the new email address in the field provided and click "Save". A confirmation email will be sent to the new address, which must be confirmed in order for the change to take effect.

**After:**

> **How to change your email address**
>
> 1. Open Settings.
> 2. Select Account.
> 3. Enter your new email address.
> 4. Select Save.
>
> We will send a confirmation email to the new address. Open it and select the link. Your email changes only after you confirm it.

**What changed:** Imperative verbs, numbered steps, one action per step. *In order to*, *subsequently*, *thereafter* are gone. The last paragraph explains the confirmation step in two short sentences instead of one long clause.

## 12. Consent request — email updates

**Before:**

> By clicking "Accept", you consent to the collection and processing of your personal data for marketing purposes, in accordance with our privacy policy. You may withdraw your consent at any time by contacting us or by following the unsubscribe link in any marketing communication you receive from us.

**After:**

> **Can we send you email updates?**
>
> We will send one email a month about new features. We will not share your email address with anyone.
>
> If you say no, you can still use everything.
>
> You can change this any time in Settings.
>
> [Yes, send updates] [Not now]

**What changed:** The question is the heading, so the reader knows what is being asked before reading further. The frequency (one a month) and the promise (no sharing) are specific. Saying no has no penalty, and the reader is told how to change the choice later.

## 13. Delay notice — delivery late

**Before:**

> We regret to inform you that, due to unforeseen circumstances beyond our control, your delivery has been delayed. We are working diligently to resolve this issue as soon as possible and we apologise for any inconvenience this may cause. We will keep you updated on the status of your delivery.

**After:**

> Your delivery will arrive 2 days late, on Friday 12 June.
>
> The delay is because of flooding on the main road to Bandung. Your parcel is safe at our depot.
>
> We will email you when it leaves the depot. You do not need to do anything.

**What changed:** The new date is in the first sentence. The reason is specific, not *unforeseen circumstances*. The reader is told the parcel is safe and that no action is needed — the two things a worried reader actually wants to know.

## 14. Empty state — no saved items

**Before:**

> You currently have no saved items. Please utilise the search functionality to find items of interest and save them for later viewing. Saved items will be displayed in this section for your convenience.

**After:**

> **Nothing saved yet**
>
> Items you save will appear here.
>
> [Search for items]

**What changed:** Four words for the title. One sentence explains the section. The button is the action. *Utilise*, *for your convenience*, and *for later viewing* — all filler — are gone.
